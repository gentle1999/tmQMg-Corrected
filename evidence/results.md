# Results and case evidence

## Upstream annotation issues

Of the 72,225 retained complexes, **4,560 meet at least one of the seven criteria below**. MolGR classified 4,915 pairs as inequivalent. The criteria identify a specific issue in 4,557 of those pairs and leave 358 unclassified. Three additional labels failed RDKit sanitization but had readable raw connectivity that exposed an input inconsistency. The chart counts each CSD ID once per category, so a molecule can appear in several rows. [Distribution](figures/upstream-issue-distribution.png) · [Per-ID flags](tables/upstream-issue-types.csv) · [Comparison verdicts](tables/equivalence-rescore.csv)

| Issue | Retained IDs | Counting criterion | Upstream and corrected graphs |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Intraligand bond order | 4,020 | In an inequivalent pair, the same ligand heavy-atom skeleton has a different distribution of integer bond orders at corresponding symmetry classes. Across copies of that skeleton, the distribution of (net charge, attached H count) pairs must also change. | [DEFWIY](figures/defwiy-3d.svg) |
| Ligand or counterion connectivity | 503 | Removing metals and hydrogens reveals a change in ligand skeleton or copy count in 436 complexes. In the other 67, the upstream graph matches the XYZ composition, but the number of hydrogens attached at corresponding ligand sites changes. | [JEGLOA](figures/jegloa-3d.svg) |
| Anomalous metal valence | 382 | Both graphs contain one metal centre of the same element. The screen adds metal-bound implicit or bracket H atoms and neutral terminal H atoms to the metal formal charge, treating these hydrogens as hydrides. The resulting upstream value falls outside the element’s combined primary and supplementary MolGR candidate sets, and the reference value falls inside. | [RAWVAN](figures/rawvan-3d.svg) |
| Charge or electron count inconsistent with input | 207 | The upstream charge differs from the input charge, the electron count differs from the input count, or the count is incompatible with the input multiplicity. Each electron count is the sum of atomic numbers for that representation minus its total charge. | [ERULUB](figures/erulub-3d.svg) |
| Atomic composition inconsistent with XYZ | 198 | The upstream element counts differ from XYZ after counting explicit H atoms, bracket H counts and valence-implied hydrogens. | [ABEVAG](figures/abevag-3d.svg) |
| Incorrect zero-valent metal | 163 | The metal has zero formal charge upstream and nonzero formal charge in the reference. In both graphs, the total charge matches the input, all metal bonds are incoming dative bonds, and the hydride adjustment leaves the metal charge unchanged. The reference has tier 1 or 2 and cites a primary report or original or revised same-ID CSD connectivity. | [SAZFEF](figures/sazfef-3d.svg) |
| Alternating charges in a conjugated system | 122 | Three consecutive heavy atoms alternate in charge sign upstream. The same symmetry classes contain a neutral three-atom path in a conjugated diene or aromatic segment of the reference. The comparison requires one distinct written ligand form on each side and a change in net charge or attached H count. | [CUDBOU](figures/cudbou-3d.svg) |

To reproduce the counts, select the IDs in `equivalence-rescore.csv` from `upstream-issue-types.csv` and count nonempty cells in each issue column. This removes 17 excluded inputs from the issue table. Ligand edits are recorded only for inequivalent pairs. Their `upstream_atoms` and `sdf_atoms` are one-based indices in their respective graphs. A site label such as `C6` groups carbons by skeleton symmetry and can represent several atoms. The metal audit gives both formal charges, terminal H counts, adjusted values and allowed states. Its flags describe assignments in the Lewis graphs.
